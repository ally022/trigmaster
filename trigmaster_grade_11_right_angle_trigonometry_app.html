Core Concept

What is Right-Angle Trigonometry?

Right-angle trigonometry is the math toolkit that connects the angles and side lengths of any triangle with a 90° right corner. If you know just 2 pieces of information (one side + one angle, or two sides), you can find any missing side or missing angle!

What does θ ("theta") mean? Don't let the Greek symbol θ scare you—it is just a nickname for a missing angle, the exact same way we use x for a missing side!
Idea 1

Labeling Sides (o, a, h)

In right triangle A-b-c (with the 90° box at corner A), stand at your active corner:

  • • h (Hypotenuse): Always the longest slanted side across from the 90° box (A). It never moves.
  • • o (Opposite): Directly across the triangle from where you are standing.
  • • a (Adjacent): Right next to your corner (touching it).
Standing at corner b: sin(b) = oh = 610
Idea 2 2-Side Matching Rule

Choosing SOH, CAH, or TOA

Ignore the blank side you don't have and don't need. Look at the 2 sides with numbers or x, and pick the only rule that uses both:

SOH Have o and h
sin(θ) = oh
CAH Have a and h
cos(θ) = ah
TOA Have o and a
tan(θ) = oa

Idea 4 Inverse Trig

Finding a Missing Angle (Why We Use Inverse Trig)

When you know 2 sides and want the angle inside, press 2nd (or Shift) to use the inverse sine (sin-1), inverse cosine (cos-1), or inverse tangent (tan-1):

Why do we use inverse trig? Because the missing angle θ is trapped inside sin(θ), cos(θ), or tan(θ), and you CANNOT divide by the word "sin" (since sin is a function, not a number). Using the inverse sine (sin-1), inverse cosine (cos-1), or inverse tangent (tan-1) acts like an "undo" button—just like subtraction undoes addition—to free the angle θ!
Example: Opposite = 5, Hypotenuse = 10
Step 1 (SOH): sin(θ) = 510
Step 2 (Use the inverse sine): θ = sin-1(510)
Step 3 (DEG mode): θ = 30°
